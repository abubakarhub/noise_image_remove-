      04 — Background

      Noise models, filters and metrics

      Noise models each swatch is a 0→255 ramp, so you can see which noise depends on brightness

      Salt-and-pepper

      g = 0 or 255 with probability d

      Impulse noise from bit errors and faulty sensor cells: scattered black and white dots, independent of the signal.

      Gaussian

      g = f + n,  n ~ N(0, σ²)

      Additive, zero-mean thermal and amplifier noise affecting every pixel equally, regardless of brightness.

      Speckle

      g = f + n·f

      Multiplicative granular noise of coherent imaging (radar, ultrasound, laser), stronger in bright regions.

      Poisson

      g ~ Poisson(f)  ·  Var = mean

      Shot noise from the discrete nature of light; dominates in low-light, microscopy and low-dose imaging.

      Filters

      Mean

      cv2.blur

      Equal-weight average of the neighbourhood. Cuts variance of additive noise, blurs edges and smears impulses.

      Median

      cv2.medianBlur

      Non-linear order statistic. Ignores a minority of outliers, so impulses vanish and step edges survive.

      Gaussian

      cv2.GaussianBlur

      Distance-weighted average; smoother and more natural than the box filter, separable and fast.

      Bilateral

      cv2.bilateralFilter

      Spatial Gaussian × intensity Gaussian: smooths within regions but not across edges. Slower, two parameters.

      Metrics

      Mean Squared Error

      MSE = (1/MN) ΣΣ [f(i,j) − g(i,j)]²

      Average squared pixel difference. Simple, but blind to how visible an error is.

      Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio

      PSNR = 10 · log₁₀(255² / MSE) dB

      MSE on a log scale. 30 dB+ is usually good for 8-bit images.

      Structural Similarity

      SSIM ∈ [−1, 1], 1 = identical

      Compares local luminance, contrast and structure, so it catches over-smoothed edges that PSNR forgives.
